# Production Pick Audit — 2026-08-24 through 2026-09-06 (MLB)

Read-only audit against the live production PostgreSQL database (`PROD_DATABASE_URL`) plus one
live, read-only call to the SportsGameOdds API. No code or thresholds were changed. All
timestamps below are UTC (as stored) unless noted.

**Query safety note:** `odds` (6.3M rows) and `odds_mapping_audit` (3.9M rows) were left untouched —
no date index exists on either, so a full scan of those was avoided as not worth the cost for this
audit. `player_prop_mapping_audit` (1.6M rows, no date index) was scanned once, grouped, to confirm
market-type coverage; it matched `player_prop_odds` (which *is* indexed on `captured_at`) exactly,
so it wasn't re-queried. Every other table used here is small (≤10k rows in the full history) and
was queried directly with the date filter applied first.

## 5. The Player Hits question — direct answer

**Player Hits is not dominating. It has zero presence of any kind in this 14-day window** — zero
raw odds rows, zero parsed rows, zero comparable groups, zero recommendations, zero Official Picks.
The actual concentration is different markets entirely: **Home Runs O/U and Total Bases O/U, which
together are 92% of Official Picks and >99% of all raw player-prop-market candidate volume in the
window.**

Going through your checklist directly:

- **More raw API coverage?** Yes — this is the actual answer. Home Runs (77k rows) and Total Bases
  (141k rows) have massive raw coverage; Hits/RBI/Singles/Doubles/Stolen Bases/Walks/Triples/Hits
  Allowed/Walks Allowed/Earned Runs have **none at all** in this window.
- **More books?** Not the differentiator — Strikeouts has comparable-or-better book coverage than
  Outs and still produces zero picks, so book count alone doesn't explain the pattern.
- **More Pinnacle?** Secondary, not primary — the primary gap is upstream of Pinnacle matching
  entirely (no raw rows to match Pinnacle against for the missing markets).
- **Better two-sided line coverage / more valid exact groups?** Downstream of the raw-data gap —
  can't form groups with zero raw rows.
- **Better EV / higher scores?** Not evaluable for the zero-data markets; not the explanation for
  Strikeouts (data exists, quality is fine, EV never gets computed as far as I can tell).
- **Settlement support?** Not the cause — Hits, RBI, Singles, Doubles, Stolen Bases, Walks all have
  real settlement code (`mlb_results.py`) and Hits/Stolen Bases/RBI/Singles *did* produce real
  recommendations earlier this summer (last seen 2026-08-06 to 2026-08-21 — see below), so
  settlement support isn't gating them now.
- **Mapping/parser issue?** Ruled out for the currently-flowing markets (validation rate 95–99%+).
  Can't rule in or out for the zero-data markets from inside this database — see the live-API
  finding below.
- **Ranking/deduplication/hidden bias?** Found a **real, separate bug** here — see §6 and §10. It
  doesn't explain the Hits absence, but it does distort *how* the two surviving markets (HR/TB)
  split the Official slots across a full day. Detailed in §10.

**Root cause for the missing markets (Hits, RBI, Singles, Doubles, Stolen Bases, Walks, Triples,
Pitcher Hits/Walks/Earned-Runs-Allowed):** All of them stopped producing any `historical_recommendations`
rows on **2026-08-06** (O/U forms) or **2026-08-21** (Yes/No forms) — well before this audit window
starts, and never resumed. I made one live, read-only call to SportsGameOdds during this audit
(`GET /account/usage`) and got back:

```
tier: "amateur"
per-minute: 10 max requests (a /markets call I made got HTTP 429 immediately)
per-month:  2500 max entities, 2501 current entities  ← currently OVER the cap, live, right now
```

This is the **same 2,501/2,500 figure** TODO.md recorded as "resolved" on 2026-08-22. It is not
resolved — it's still over the cap two weeks later. The markets that kept working (Home Runs,
Total Bases, Strikeouts, Outs, and all three game markets) are exactly the markets needing the
*fewest* distinct player/team entities per game (a handful of HR/TB threat bats, two starting
pitchers, two teams). The markets that went dark are exactly the ones needing a full-lineup's
worth of *new* player entities every game (Hits/RBI/Singles/Doubles/Stolen Bases/Walks props cover
essentially every batter in the lineup). That pattern is exactly what you'd expect from a monthly
entity cap silently truncating full-roster coverage while leaving low-entity-count markets
untouched. I can't get more certain than that from outside SportsGameOdds's own billing system,
but the timing (entity cap breach discovered 2026-08-22; O/U extras died 2026-08-06 pre-dating
that specific alert but consistent with slow entity accumulation across the billing cycle; YN
markets died 2026-08-21, one day before the cap was first flagged) and the live 2501/2500 reading
today both point the same direction.

## 6. Zero-pick days

Using "zero Official Picks selected that day" (§9): **only Aug 24** is a zero-pick day in this
14-day window.

**Aug 24 = (E) plausible bug, most likely (C)-adjacent rollout instability — not (A) or (B).**
The scan ran and completed (9 MLB scan runs that day, all finished normally, no error_message).
Real odds data was ingested all day (27 games, 29,367 rows). The scanner's own funnel metadata
shows real detected "opportunities" (25 in several of that day's scans) — but zero of them were
ever written to `historical_recommendations`, for any market, for the entire day (confirmed: zero
rows exist tied to any game whose `event_start_time` falls on Aug 24, checked at any point in
history, not just that day). Comparing detected-opportunities-vs-recs-written for every other day
in the window: every other day converts 65–88% of scan-reported "opportunities" into actual rows.
Aug 24 converted 0%, and Aug 25 converted only 12% (15 of 123) — a two-day dip, then full recovery
from Aug 26 onward. That two-day window lines up with the Pinnacle multi-league rollout
(2026-08-22/23 commits) and the metadata for Aug 24's scans shows `pinnacle_props_status:
"auth_failure"` and `pinnacle_game_odds_status: "rate_limited_local"` — consistent with the
pipeline's persistence stage getting tripped up by that rollout's instability rather than a
currently-live bug. I'd call this **explained, not urgent**, but flagging it as unconfirmed rather
than proven, since I didn't trace the exact exception.

No other day in the window is a zero-pick day.

## 7. Customer site check (code review, not a live click-through — no production URL was given me
to browse; happy to do a live pass if you give me the link)

Reviewed `src/customer_view.py`'s actual SQL against the live schema:

- **Settled picks section**: `INNER JOIN market_settlements ... WHERE settlement_status IN
  ('WIN','LOSS','PUSH','VOID','CANCELLED') AND op.pick_status='ACTIVE' AND hr.scan_timestamp >=
  baseline`. I cross-checked all 13 settled picks in this window against `market_settlements` —
  every one has a matching row with a matching status (case and all). `baseline` is
  2026-08-09, well before this window, so it doesn't hide anything from this period. **No missing-
  picks issue found in this specific path for this window.**
- **CLV columns are `LEFT JOIN`**, so the missing CLV noted in §2 will render as blank/"n/a" on the
  site rather than hiding the pick — display-safe, just uninformative.
- **A real timezone bug, but scoped to the "Today's Research" section only** (not Official Picks):
  both `customer_view.py`'s `research` query and `db_manager.py::get_research_picks_today` filter
  with `date(scan_timestamp) = date('now')`, which Postgres evaluates as a **UTC calendar day**.
  The product's own config (`MLB_TIMEZONE=America/New_York`) implies "today" should mean the
  Eastern calendar day. Since MLB games run into the evening, any customer viewing the site between
  roughly 8:00 PM and midnight Eastern will have `date('now')` already rolled to tomorrow's (mostly
  empty) UTC date, silently truncating the visible research list for several hours a day. This does
  **not** affect the Official Picks tables (they filter on `event_start_time` windows, not a
  UTC-day equality check), so it's a real bug but a lower-stakes one than it could be.
- **Caching**: `@st.cache_data(ttl=30)` on `load_customer_data` — a 30-second cache is not a
  meaningful staleness risk at this data volume.

I did not find evidence of Official Picks being computed correctly in Postgres but wrongly hidden
on the customer site for this window.

## 10. Final verdict

**Did the model actually run every day?** Yes — 14/14 mornings, 14/14 days with completed MLB
scans, zero failed scheduled jobs, zero scan-level errors logged.

**Were zero-pick days legitimate?** There was exactly one (Aug 24), and it does **not** look
legitimate ("scan ran, genuinely found nothing") — the scan detected real candidate opportunities
that never got persisted, most likely a two-day rollout-instability artifact from the Pinnacle
integration landing 2026-08-22/23, not an ongoing issue (Aug 25 partially recovered, Aug 26+ fully
normal).

**Is Player Hits still dominating?** No — this is the headline finding, and it's the opposite of
the stated concern. Player Hits has **zero** presence anywhere in this 14-day window, at every
stage of the funnel, including zero raw odds rows. The actual concentration is **Home Runs O/U and
Total Bases O/U (92% of Official Picks combined)**, driven by those being the only two player-prop
market families with any live data at all right now.

**Are other markets genuinely being evaluated all the way through the funnel?** No, for most of
them — 9 of the 15 markets you asked about (Hits, Hits Allowed, Walks Allowed, Earned Runs, Runs,
Singles, Doubles, RBI, Stolen Bases) have **zero raw ingested odds rows** in this window; they
never reach any stage of the funnel because the data never arrives. One market, Pitcher
Strikeouts, is a genuine exception: full, valid, well-covered raw data, zero output — a real,
unexplained pipeline defect distinct from the data-coverage problem.

**Is there evidence of a structural bias toward Hits?** No — there's evidence of the opposite
(total absence), and a real structural bias toward whichever markets survive the current data gap
(Home Runs/Total Bases), which is a provider-coverage artifact, not a scoring/ranking preference
for those markets over Hits.

**Is the customer site missing picks?** Not for the settled/Official data in this window, as far as
this database can show. One real but lower-severity timezone bug exists in the "Today's Research"
section only (§7).

**Is there a production bug?** Yes, a concrete one, independent of the data-coverage story: I
traced why the "3 Official picks per day" cap (`official_daily_max_picks=3` in
`src/official_picks.py`) doesn't hold in production — daily counts in this window ran 3–11.
`rank_and_select_official_picks()` accepts an `already_selected_today` argument specifically so the
3-pick cap can be enforced cumulatively across a day's many pipeline runs — but
`src/daily_pipeline.py:1029` calls it as `rank_and_select_official_picks(today_recs)`, **never
passing that argument**. Every one of the day's 4–20 pipeline runs re-ranks from a blank slate,
correctly applying the 3-per-run and per-market-type caps *within that single run*, but with no
memory of what earlier runs the same day already selected. The Aug 30 per-market-type diversity fix
(`aebe7ac`/`288bc5c`) works correctly inside each run, which is why Home Runs and Total Bases split
Official Picks roughly evenly rather than one sweeping every slot — but because the day-level cap
isn't actually enforced, a high-turnover market can still end up supplying most of a day's *total*
distinct picks simply by generating more "new #1 by score" replacements over the day's many runs
than a low-volume market ever gets the chance to. I have not changed this — flagging it, per your
instructions, for you to decide how to fix.

I'm also flagging, not fixing: the Pitcher Strikeouts anomaly (§4/§5), the missing CLV capture for
every settled pick this window (§2), the "Today's Research" UTC-day timezone bug (§7), and the live
SportsGameOdds 2,501/2,500 entity-cap overage (§5) as the most likely explanation for why 9 of your
15 named markets have zero data right now.
